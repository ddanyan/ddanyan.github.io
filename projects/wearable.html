Wrist-use feedback wearable

Context
UW neuroengineering · Four-person team lead
Status
Demonstrated capstone prototype
Scope
EMG + inertial sensing

Outcome & boundary
Functioning sensing and feedback prototype demonstrated. Long-term accuracy and clinical effectiveness were not established.

Challenge

Combine forearm muscle activity and wrist posture sensing in a wearable prototype that provides timely feedback during repetitive wrist use.

Role & scope

I led the four-person team, independently designed the enclosure in Fusion 360 and created the embedded electrical schematic. I helped develop the monitoring and feedback concept. Teammates developed embedded firmware and the companion application.

Technical approach

A MyoWare 2.0 sensor supplied processed EMG output. Two IMUs on the hand and wrist estimated relative orientation. A cumulative activity indicator decreased during sustained muscle activation, with additional penalties for unfavorable wrist angles.

Implementation

An ESP32-S3-based architecture integrated the sensors, lithium-polymer battery, boost converter, RGB LED and vibration motor. A PLA enclosure and reusable sleeve supported the hardware. The application displayed the activity indicator and wrist-use status.

Verification & results

The team demonstrated a functioning prototype at the final capstone evaluation, including muscle-activity monitoring, orientation sensing and feedback. Sensitivity was adjusted to illustrate alert behavior within the short demonstration period.

Trade-offs & next steps

Long-term accuracy, clinical effectiveness and prevention of carpal tunnel syndrome were not established. This was a sensing and feedback prototype, not a validated diagnostic or preventive medical device. Enclosure refinement and longer-duration evaluation remain potential next steps.
